01

The idea

A table seen from above
Eight places around a table. A cabinet is a small group that runs something together, and the kit gives a project exactly that: roles with clear authority.
Seven seats are filled
The agents hold the seats. Each decides within its own authority and hands work to the next.
One place is left open
The eighth place belongs to the owner, who signs off on what is irreversible, public or costs money. The gap is also what turns the ring into a C.
02

The mark

Seven dots on a ring, the open place on the right. One mark at every size, in one color.

Three forms

Primary

Dark seats

On white and light backgrounds. The default everywhere the background is known to be light.

Reversed

Light seats

On dark backgrounds: slides, the app icon, dark pages.

Tile

On its own tile

Where the page can be light or dark and you cannot choose the file, such as a README. It reads the same on both.

Sizes

The same drawing from 16px up. There is no simplified small version, and none is needed.

Clear space

Keep one seat's width clear on every side. Nothing else enters that space, including the name.

1 seat

Construction

On a 100 unit grid: eight places every 45 degrees on a ring of radius 36, seats 19 units wide, the place at 0 degrees left open.

03

The open seat in brass

A brass outline in the gap marks the place kept for the owner. It is an accent, not a second logo: use it where the owner is the subject and the color will show.

Use the outline

Where the owner acts

  • The daily brief and the sign-off queue, where the owner decides.
  • Onboarding and welcome pages, and slides about how the owner signs off.
  • The app icon and large headers, at 48px or more, in full color.
Use the plain mark

Everywhere else (the default)

  • Small sizes: favicons and anything under 48px, where a thin outline blurs.
  • One color: the terminal, one-color print, stickers, embossing.
  • Next to the name: the README, the npm page and the wordmark, where the job is to be recognized.

Brass on a light background is #A06F17; on a dark one it is #D7A54B. The outline is never filled, and never any other color.

04

The name

Always cabinet, in lowercase, also at the start of a sentence, the way the command is typed. Never "Cabinet", "CABINET" or "the Cabinet kit".

05

Color

Mostly ink and paper. Brass is rare on purpose: it only ever means the owner.

How much of each

Paper and white about 60%, ink 30%, slate and line 9%, brass at most 1%.

07

Writing

cabinet sounds like a capable chief of staff: plain, short, specific, and always clear about who decides.

  • Plain words. Say what happens and who does it. Name things the way the owner sees them, not how they are built.
  • The fix comes with the problem. A message that reports a problem ends with the command that fixes it.
  • ASCII punctuation. No em or en dashes, no curly quotes.
  • Dates and times people read as 18 Oct 2026 and 2:30 PM. Machine formats stay ISO.
  • No comparisons with other products. Name other tools only as facts, such as which agent tools have guardrails.
  • The owner decides. Agents recommend; the owner signs off. Never write as if an agent shipped, merged or published.

Words we use

WordMeansNot

roleAn agent's job: what it owns, decides, escalates and may write.bot, persona

sign-off queueEvery decision waiting on the owner, at the top of the brief.inbox, approvals
briefThe daily summary in BRIEF.md.report, digest (a digest gathers every queue)
guardrailsRules that keep push, merge, release and publish with the owner.restrictions, blocks

Text

Terminal

cabinet version prints the mark in a terminal: seven lines, because a terminal cell is about twice as tall as it is wide. Without a UTF-8 locale the seats are the letter o.

Reversed, one color

Sticker and print

One color only, on ink or on paper. No brass: it does not reproduce reliably.
