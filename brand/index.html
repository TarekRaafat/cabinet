01 Approved

The idea

What Cabinet believes
  • People amplified, not replaced. Every person becomes a whole team, or a whole company. Agents bring the skills and the hours; the person brings vision, judgment, and the final say.
  • More output per person, not more headcount.
  • A small company, not a corporate org chart. Every piece of work has exactly one owner, and nothing is done twice.
  • People decide, agents do the work. Agents recommend; you sign off on anything irreversible, public or costly, and you always know what is happening.
  • For anyone. One person or a team of any size, building anything, working their own way.

Every rule in this guide follows from these. The open seat is the person who decides; the seven seats are the work done for them.

A table seen from above
Eight places around a table. A cabinet is a small group that runs something together, and the kit gives a project exactly that: roles with clear authority.
Seven seats are filled
The agents hold the seats. Each decides within its own authority and hands work to the next.
One place is left open
The eighth place belongs to the owner, who signs off on what is irreversible, public or costs money. The gap is also what turns the ring into a C.
The table

For explaining, never for the logo

  • Use it in pictures that explain the idea: this one, onboarding, an explainer video, a slide about how the team works, and with the chairs, where it makes the meeting obvious.
  • Never in the mark itself: the logo, favicon, app icon, README, wordmark or terminal. A filled center competes with the open place, and at 16 to 32px it reads as a blob.
  • Always faint: the seats' color at 12%, never brass, and well inside the ring.

The mark

Seven dots on a ring, the open place on the right. One mark at every size, in one color.

Three forms

Reversed

Tile

On its own tile

Where the page can be light or dark and you cannot choose the file, such as a README. It reads the same on both.

Sizes

The same drawing from 16px up. There is no simplified small version, and none is needed.

Clear space

Keep one seat's width clear on every side. Nothing else enters that space, including the name.

1 seat

Construction

On a 100 unit grid: eight places every 45 degrees on a ring of radius 36, seats 19 units wide, the place at 0 degrees left open.

Loading: the team at work Approved

The seats stay in place and light up one at a time, counterclockwise from the top, like a word passed around the table. The open place is a rest beat: nothing lights there. When the work is done, every seat comes up to full and it ends on the mark.

Loading...
How it moves
  • One beat every 150 ms, eight beats a turn (1.2 s), the open place included. The lit seat fades back to 30% over two beats, eased out.
  • Only opacity changes. Nothing turns, moves, grows or changes color. No brass: loading is not the owner's moment.
  • Done, or failed: every seat comes up to full over 400 ms. A failure says what went wrong in words.
  • After 400 ms only, so a short wait never flashes. When the progress is known, show the progress instead.
Not
  • More than one on a screen, on the header logo, or anywhere nothing is loading: the README, slides, print.
  • For a launch screen: a wait that opens something plays the logo animation, which starts with the owner. Loading is the one motion without the owner.
  • Without words: it says "Loading" to screen readers, and past a few seconds a line says what is happening. With reduced motion it is the still mark and the word.

03 Approved

The open seat in brass

A brass outline in the gap marks the place kept for the owner. It is an accent, not a second logo: use it where the owner is the subject and the color will show.

Use the outline

Where the owner acts

  • The daily brief and the sign-off queue, where the owner decides.
  • Onboarding and welcome pages, and slides about how the owner signs off.
  • The app icon and large headers, at 48px or more, in full color.
Use the plain mark

Everywhere else (the default)

  • Small sizes: favicons and anything under 48px, where a thin outline blurs.
  • One color: the terminal, one-color print, stickers, embossing.
  • Next to the name: the README, the npm page and the wordmark, where the job is to be recognized.

On hover: the seat appears when the owner reaches for it

Where the mark can be pointed at or clicked, the brass outline may fade in on hover and keyboard focus, and fade out after. It is the outline rule in motion: the owner's place shows when a person engages. Try it on the mark below or on the cover.

Use it
  • The logo in a website or docs header, where it links home: the hover shows it can be clicked.
  • Pages only the owner opens, such as the owner's manual page (OPERATIONS.html has no logo yet).
  • Onboarding and welcome screens, played once when the owner arrives instead of on hover, like frame 3 of the chairs.
Not
  • Where nothing can respond: the README, npm, social previews, slides, print.
  • On every mark on a page. One logo with the hover, at most; more wears out its meaning.
  • With anything else moving. Only the outline's opacity changes, over 400 ms. With reduced motion it appears without the fade. Touch screens have no hover, so the mark stays plain there.

Brass on a light background is #A06F17; on a dark one it is #D7A54B. The outline is never filled, and never any other color.

  • Nothing turns. The owner starts in the middle and ends in the open place on the right.
  • The open place is never taken by an agent's chair. Only the owner's.
  • Calm timing: each step 400 to 800 ms, eased in and out, no bounce. It moves in motion.html, "Pull up a chair".
  • Reduced motion: where people have turned motion off, show the last frame only.
Use the chairs

To tell the story, at 96px or more

  • Animation and video: intros, explainers, the end card.
  • Onboarding, welcome pages and empty states.
  • Docs and slides that explain the team; a chair can carry a role's name beside it.
  • With the table in the middle when it helps the story, faint as in "The idea".
  • A website's opening picture, posters and other large print.
Never

As the logo, or small

  • As the favicon, app icon, avatar or README logo, or next to the name in a lockup.
  • Under 96px, where the backrests blur.
  • With more or fewer than eight places, or in any color but ink, mist and the owner's brass.

06 Approved

Color

Mostly ink and paper. Brass is rare on purpose: it only ever means the owner.

How much of each

Paper and white about 60%, ink 30%, slate and line 9%, brass at most 1%.

Pages, light and dark

A page follows the reader's setting, with no toggle. Every color is a token, or a token at an opacity. On dark: cards are mist at 4% over night, secondary text mist at 65%, dividers mist at 14%, control borders mist at 50%.

Light: a card on paper
secondary text control

paper, white, ink, slate (5.5:1), line; the owner's place in brass

Dark: a card on night
secondary text control

night, mist, mist at 65% (7:1), 14% and 50%; the owner's place in brass-light

Status colors

One color for each level cabinet doctor reports: ok, note and fix. The warning is blue, not amber or yellow, so it can never be mistaken for brass, and a note is for your information, not an alarm. Anything urgent is a fix. Status colors are not counted in the shares above: they appear only where there is a status.

  • The word always comes with the color: ok, note, fix. About 1 in 12 men cannot tell red from green, so color supports the word and never replaces it.
  • Status colors only mark status. Never buttons, links, headings or decoration, just as brass only marks the owner.
  • One status color per line. A line is one finding.
  • In a terminal, use the terminal's own red, green and blue, and never yellow for a status: yellow stands in for brass and marks only what waits on the owner. Color only when the output is a terminal, and none when NO_COLOR is set.

08 Approved

Writing

Cabinet sounds like a capable chief of staff: plain, short, specific, and always clear about who decides.

  • Plain words. Say what happens and who does it. Name things the way the owner sees them, not how they are built.
  • The fix comes with the problem. A message that reports a problem ends with the command that fixes it.
  • ASCII punctuation. No em or en dashes, no curly quotes.
  • Dates and times people read as 18 Oct 2026 and 2:30 PM. Machine formats stay ISO.
  • No comparisons with other products. Name other tools only as facts, such as which agent tools have guardrails.
  • The owner decides. Agents recommend; the owner signs off. Never write as if an agent shipped, merged or published.
